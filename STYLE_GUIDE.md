# Meridian Editorial Style Guide

## Purpose

Meridian helps people navigate consequential decisions through clear explanations, balanced analysis, and practical recommendations.

Every guide should leave the reader more capable of making a decision than they were before arriving.

## Core Standard

Every guide must answer one primary decision.

Examples:

- Is this premium credit card worth its annual fee?
- Should I form an LLC for my small business?
- Should I lease or purchase this equipment?
- Is this software subscription worth paying for?
- Should I downgrade or cancel this credit card?

Avoid articles that only define a topic without helping the reader act.

## Meridian Guide Structure

Every guide should follow this sequence:

1. Category
2. Title
3. Description
4. Updated date and reading time
5. Decision Snapshot
6. Bottom Line
7. Recommended For
8. What You’ll Learn
9. Why This Decision Matters
10. Options and Tradeoffs
11. Recommendation or decision framework
12. Key Takeaways
13. Sources
14. Continue Exploring

Sections may be adjusted when the subject requires it, but the decision should remain central.

## Editorial Principles

### Clarity over complexity

Use straightforward language. Explain specialized terms when they are necessary.

### Usefulness over volume

Do not add paragraphs merely to make a guide longer. Every section should help the reader understand, compare, decide, or act.

### Accuracy over speed

Verify factual claims before publication. Do not invent figures, quotes, citations, examples, or firsthand experience.

### Recommendations before exposition

Give the reader the Bottom Line near the beginning. Then explain the reasoning.

### Real value over advertised value

When evaluating products, services, credits, rewards, or benefits, distinguish between theoretical value and value the reader is likely to realize.

### Balanced tradeoffs

Present legitimate advantages and disadvantages. Do not disguise marketing copy as analysis.

### Transparency about uncertainty

State when an answer depends on assumptions, incomplete data, changing rules, or individual circumstances.

### Human accountability

AI may assist with research, outlining, drafting, and editing. A human must review each guide for accuracy, usefulness, tone, and misleading claims before publication.

## Voice

Meridian should sound:

- Calm
- Practical
- Direct
- Credible
- Thoughtful
- Nonjudgmental

Meridian should not sound:

- Sensational
- Promotional
- Overly casual
- Academic for its own sake
- Certain when the evidence is uncertain
- Like generic AI-generated content

## Writing Rules

### Titles

Use natural decision-focused titles.

Preferred:

- Is Your Premium Credit Card Worth the Annual Fee?
- Should You Form an LLC for Your Side Business?
- Should You Lease or Buy Equipment?

Avoid:

- The Ultimate Complete Guide to Everything About Premium Credit Cards
- 17 Shocking Reasons You Need an LLC
- Best Credit Cards You Must Get Today

### Bottom Line

The Bottom Line should:

- Answer the decision directly
- State the most important conditions
- Remain concise
- Avoid burying the recommendation in qualifications

Example:

> Keep the card if you naturally use enough of its recurring credits and rewards to exceed the annual fee. Otherwise, downgrade to a lower-fee option.

### Paragraphs

Prefer short paragraphs of two to four sentences.

Use longer paragraphs only when a complete argument requires them.

### Headings

Headings should describe the reader’s question or decision.

Preferred:

- Why This Decision Matters
- What the Card Really Costs
- When Keeping the Card Makes Sense
- When Downgrading Is Better
- How to Calculate Your Real Value

Avoid vague headings such as:

- Introduction
- More Information
- Other Things
- Conclusion

### Lists

Use lists when they improve scanning.

Do not convert every paragraph into bullets.

### Tables

Use tables for direct comparisons such as:

- Costs
- Benefits
- Options
- Eligibility
- Timelines
- Tradeoffs

Tables should simplify a decision, not merely decorate the page.

## Evidence and Sources

Use authoritative and primary sources whenever possible.

Examples:

- Government agencies
- Issuers and manufacturers
- Official product documentation
- Research papers
- Regulatory bodies
- Original datasets

Secondary sources may be used for context, interpretation, or competing viewpoints.

Every factual claim that may change over time should be verified before publication.

Never fabricate citations.

## Financial Content

Financial guides are educational and should not be presented as individualized financial advice.

Clearly distinguish:

- Facts
- Estimates
- Assumptions
- Opinions
- Recommendations

Avoid guarantees about approval, returns, savings, credit scores, or financial outcomes.

## Firsthand Experience

Do not imply that Meridian personally tested, purchased, visited, or used something unless that occurred.

Use honest phrasing:

- Based on the published fee structure...
- According to the issuer’s terms...
- Using the assumptions below...
- For a reader who spends approximately...

Do not write:

- We tested every card...
- In our experience...
- We personally found...

unless those claims are true.

## Original Value Requirement

Every guide should add at least one element beyond a generic summary.

Examples:

- Decision framework
- Calculator
- Worked example
- Comparison table
- Checklist
- Original analysis
- Downloadable worksheet
- Decision tree
- Interview
- Original data

## Review Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- [ ] The guide answers one clear decision.
- [ ] The Bottom Line appears near the beginning.
- [ ] The recommendation includes its important conditions.
- [ ] The options and tradeoffs are presented fairly.
- [ ] Factual claims have been verified.
- [ ] Time-sensitive information is current.
- [ ] No citations, numbers, or quotes were invented.
- [ ] The article does not claim firsthand experience that did not occur.
- [ ] The guide adds original value.
- [ ] The next action is clear.
- [ ] Related guides are relevant.
- [ ] The page has been reviewed by a human.

## Brand Terminology

Use:

- Meridian — public-facing publication
- Northward Meridian — company or publisher
- Guides — published decision-focused resources
- Decision Snapshot — the summary near the beginning
- Bottom Line — the concise recommendation
- Continue Exploring — related guide recommendations

Avoid referring to guides as blog posts.
